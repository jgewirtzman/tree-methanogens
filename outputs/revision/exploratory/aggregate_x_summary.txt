KEY FINDING (ratio predictor):

On RAW flux, individual and aggregate-x fits are NON-significant and small:
  M1 individual           slope=0.0293 R2=0.009 p=0.305
  M3 aggregate-x chamber  slope=0.0284 R2=0.001 p=0.509
  -> NOT because x-attenuation is absent, but because raw flux is heavily
     right-skewed: a few extreme emission events (unrelated to the ratio)
     dominate the mean-based OLS. cor(x, species MEDIAN flux)=0.717 but
     cor(x, species MEAN flux)=0.328. The species result uses the MEDIAN.

On PSEUDO-LOG flux (valid OLS scale; the manuscript already uses this for
individual flux display), the relationship strengthens as x is aggregated:
  M1 individual           slope=0.0448 R2=0.026 p=0.078  <- MARGINAL (weak, ~0.08)
  M3 aggregate-x chamber  slope=0.0756 R2=0.011 p=0.028  <- SIGNIFICANT, de-biased
  M3b aggregate-x 125cm   slope=0.1634 R2=0.054 p=0.013  <- SIGNIFICANT
  M2 species SMA          slope=0.1528 R2=0.510 p=0.020
  -> aggregating the noisy predictor moves the fit from marginal (individual)
     to significant (aggregate-x), i.e. x-attenuation IS operating once the
     y-scale is appropriate. Aggregation sharpens rather than manufactures
     the signal (the individual fit is already marginally positive, not zero).

IMPLICATION for the manuscript: the 'individual R2<0.001, no individual
relationship' claim is largely an artifact of raw heavy-tailed flux. A more
defensible framing: a weak (marginal) individual relationship, strongly
attenuated by within-tree heterogeneity AND flux skew, that sharpens to a
robust species-level (species-typical/median) association. This removes the
'how can species work if individual is exactly zero' paradox R3 pressed on.
