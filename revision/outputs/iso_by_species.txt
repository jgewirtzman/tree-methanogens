=================================================================
ISOTOPE SOURCE AGGREGATION — is it one source or species-specific?
=================================================================

SETUP: whole-tree = ~1 sample per tree, so the pooled Keeling/Miller-Tans
concentration axis is BETWEEN-tree variation. The pooled source is an
ECOSYSTEM/POPULATION-AVERAGE signature, NOT a within-tree source.

*** THRESHOLD SENSITIVITY (the decisive test) ***
Low-CH4 samples have unreliable d13C. Is the species difference real?

=> The among-species difference is significant ONLY at the lowest threshold
   (>=1.5 ppm, p~0.02, d13C IQR ~27) and DISAPPEARS at >=5-10 ppm (p>0.18)

   CONCENTRATION-DEPENDENT NOISE ARTIFACT, not distinct species sources.

CORRECTED ANSWER (do we aggregate or divide?):
 1. AGGREGATE is appropriate. At concentrations where d13C is reliable
    (>=5-10 ppm), species converge on one depleted source -- no significant
    among-species heterogeneity. The pooled Miller-Tans (concentration-
    weighted) is robust BECAUSE it downweights the noisy low-CH4 samples.
 2. FRAME the pooled source as an ecosystem/population-average (whole-tree
    is ~1 sample/tree, so the mixing line is between-tree, not within-tree).
 3. eps_C uses BULK internal CO2 (mostly respiration), NOT the methanogenic
    substrate CO2 -> APPARENT fractionation; caveat it (R2's L607-610 point).
 4. For a TRUE within-sample source of PRODUCED CH4, the incubation time-
    series (V*_H1..H48) are the right tool -- but that is a separate dataset
    (likely a separate paper); flag for Jon, don't fold in here.
