# Panel (a) taxa — grouping, evidence, and verified citations

Panel (a) shows the 5 families whose relative abundance is significantly associated with
heartwood *mcrA* (LMM, FDR < 0.05; = Fig. S6 strict). Grouped into three evidence-graded
categories, coloured red / gold / grey:

| Family | Group (colour) | What the evidence actually shows |
|---|---|---|
| *Methanobacteriaceae* | Methanogen (red) | Hydrogenotrophic methanogen; H₂/formate + CO₂ → CH₄ |
| *Methanomassiliicoccaceae* | Methanogen (red) | Obligate H₂-dependent methyl-reducing methanogen |
| *Christensenellaceae* | Associate / syntroph (gold) | **Demonstrated** interspecies H₂ transfer to a methanogen in defined co-culture |
| *Dysgonomonadaceae* | Associate / syntroph (gold) | **Community-level** association; fermenter/acetate-oxidizer supplying methanogen substrates |
| *Eggerthellaceae* | Other fermenter (grey) | Amino-acid fermenter (arginine→ATP); **no established net-H₂ edge**; can consume H₂ |

All citations below were verified by web lookup (July 2026) — DOIs confirmed.

## Draft discussion sentence (drop-in)

> The families co-occurring with *mcrA* span a gradient of evidence for feeding methanogenesis.
> The strongest case is *Christensenellaceae*, for which interspecies H₂ transfer to a
> hydrogenotrophic methanogen has been demonstrated directly in defined co-culture and shown to
> underlie the two taxa's global co-occurrence (Ruaud et al. 2020; Goodrich et al. 2014; Waters &
> Ley 2019). *Dysgonomonadaceae* (e.g. *Proteiniphilum*) are documented community-level associates
> of methanogens in methanogenic systems, where they act as fermenters and syntrophic acetate
> oxidizers supplying methanogenic substrates, though a specific obligate partnership has not been
> established (Feng et al. 2022; Hahnke et al. 2016). *Eggerthellaceae*, by contrast, are amino-acid
> fermenters that generate ATP via the arginine deiminase pathway and are not established net H₂
> producers — some members instead consume H₂ as a reductant (Noecker et al. 2023) — so we treat
> their co-occurrence with *mcrA* as consistent with a shared anaerobic niche rather than direct
> syntrophic support. The two methanogen families are themselves H₂/methyl-dependent
> (*Methanobacteriaceae*, hydrogenotrophic; *Methanomassiliicoccaceae*, obligate H₂-dependent
> methylotrophic; Cozannet et al. 2021; Borrel et al. 2013), reinforcing that the co-occurring
> community is organized around H₂/C1 flow to methanogenesis.

## Caption note (short form, if space-limited)

> Colour: red = methanogen; gold = fermentative associate with evidence of feeding methanogenesis
> (demonstrated H₂ syntrophy for *Christensenellaceae*, community-level association for
> *Dysgonomonadaceae*); grey = fermenter co-occurring in the same anaerobic niche without an
> established H₂ link (*Eggerthellaceae*).
